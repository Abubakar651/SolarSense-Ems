# Verification of LESCO Residential Electricity Tariffs, Billing Architecture, Prosumer Net-Billing Rules, and Metering Infrastructure in Lahore (CY 2026)

## Executive Regulatory Overview and Calendar-Year Rebasing Architecture

The residential retail electricity tariff structure governing Lahore Electric Supply Company ([LESCO](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.lesco.gov.pk/)) underwent two foundational structural transformations during the first quarter of calendar year 2026. First, the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority ([NEPRA](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php)) transitioned the national tariff determination cycle from the historical fiscal-year schedule (July to June) to a calendar-year rebasing framework effective 1 January 2026. On 7 January 2026, the Authority issued its comprehensive determination for LESCO's revised annual rebasing for Calendar Year 2026 ([Case No. NEPRA/TRF-603 & TRF-604, Decision dated 07-01-2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF)) alongside the National Power Purchase Price (PPP) forecast determination for the Central Power Purchasing Agency ([Case No. NEPRA/TRF-100/CPPAG, Decision dated 07-01-2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/DISCOs/Rebasing%20Decisions/TRF-100%20CPPAG%20FOR%20APPROVAL%20OF%20POWER%20PURCHASE%20PRICE%20FORECAST%2007-01-2026%20437-76.PDF)). Following a motion filed by the Federal Government under Sections 7 and 31 of the NEPRA Act, NEPRA approved the Uniform Consumer-End Tariff on 12 January 2026 ([Decision on Motion of Federal Govt. No. 1004-23 dated 12-01-2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/Decision%20of%20the%20Authority%20Motion%20of%20Federal%20Govt.%201004-23%20dated%2012.01.2026.PDF)), which the Ministry of Energy (Power Division) notified in the Official Gazette on 13 January 2026 via Statutory Regulatory Orders S.R.O. 41(I)/2026 through S.R.O. 52(I)/2026, with [S.R.O. 46(I)/2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php) specifically governing LESCO.

Second, less than one month into the new calendar-year schedule, the Federal Government filed a tariff rationalization motion designed to reduce the cross-subsidy burden borne by industrial consumers (approximately Rs 131 billion nationwide) by restructuring residential fixed charges and slightly trimming upper-slab residential variable rates. NEPRA approved this cross-subsidy rationalization on 11 February 2026 ([NEPRA Tariff Rationalization Decision dated 11-02-2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-100%20XWDISCOS%20AND%20KE%20RATIONALIZATION%20OF%20TARIFF%2011-02-2026%202935-57.pdf)), and the Power Division notified the revised Schedule of Tariff on 12 February 2026 via [S.R.O. 279(I)/2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php) (within the S.R.O. 274(I)/2026 to S.R.O. 284(I)/2026 series), as corroborated by [Business Recorder (12-02-2026)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.brecorder.com/news/40406877/nepra-approves-rs404unit-tariff-cut-for-industry) and [The Express Tribune (14-02-2026)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://tribune.com.pk/story/2592549/govt-notifies-new-power-tariffs-fixed-charges). Consequently, the base Schedule of Tariff established in July 2025 under [S.R.O. 1161(I)/2025 dated 01-07-2025](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2025/07%20Jul/SRO%201161%2001-07-2025%20LESCO.pdf) and the initial January 2026 notification ([S.R.O. 46(I)/2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php)) have been superseded in respect of residential fixed charges, unprotected energy charges above 300 units, and Time-of-Use (ToU) off-peak energy charges.

Simultaneously, on 9 February 2026, NEPRA promulgated the [NEPRA (Prosumer) Regulations, 2026 via S.R.O. 251(I)/2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/NEPRA%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%2520%2528SRO%2520251%2528I%25292026%2529%252009-02-26.PDF), repealing the decade-old 2015 Net Metering Regulations and replacing one-to-one unit netting with gross net-billing at the National Average Energy Purchase Price (NAEPP) for new distributed generation applicants, before issuing a critical grandfathering amendment via [S.R.O. 547(I)/2026 dated 02-04-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/S.R.O%2520547%25281%2529%25202026%2520notification%2520amendment%2520in%2520NEPRA%2520%2528Prosumer%2529%2520Regulations%252002-04-2026.PDF) and a 25 kW concurrence exemption via [S.R.O. 1330(I)/2026 dated 06-08-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/SRO%25201330%2528I%2529-2026%2520Dated%252006-08-2026.pdf). For any solar and battery energy-management system (EMS) operating in Lahore, these overlapping tariff and regulatory shifts fundamentally alter dispatch optimization, peak-shaving economics, and self-consumption vs. grid-export valuation.

### Explanation of Primary vs. Secondary Source Discrepancies Across Pakistani Tariff Literature

A recurring challenge when auditing Pakistani electricity tariff data is that primary regulatory determinations and secondary consumer portals frequently publish seemingly divergent figures for the same residential slab. Rigorous cross-examination of primary NEPRA schedules reveals four systematic reasons for these discrepancies:

1. **NEPRA Determined Uniform Tariff vs. Government-Notified Applicable Tariff**: Every Schedule of Tariff (SoT) contains two parallel sets of columns: the *NEPRA Determined Uniform Tariff* (reflecting full cost-of-service recovery with and without Prior Year Adjustments, which in the [12-01-2026 Uniform Tariff Decision](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/Decision%20of%20the%20Authority%20Motion%20of%20Federal%20Govt.%201004-23%20dated%2012.01.2026.PDF) stands at Rs 36.13/kWh across all slabs up to 300 units) and the *Government of Pakistan (GoP) Applicable Uniform Tariff* (the subsidized or cross-subsidized rate actually billed to consumers after applying Tariff Differential Subsidies). Furthermore, LESCO's standalone cost-of-service determination ([07-01-2026 LESCO Rebasing Decision, Annex-III, p. 17](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF)) lists LESCO-specific cost-of-service rates (e.g., Rs 31.14/kWh across slabs up to 300 units, Rs 28.01/kWh Peak, and Rs 25.62/kWh Off-Peak) prior to national uniformization. Consumer billing calculators use the GoP Applicable column, whereas regulatory analyses of utility revenue requirements cite the NEPRA Determined column. Both values are presented side-by-side throughout this report.
2. **Superseded Notifications (July 2025 vs. January 2026 vs. February 2026)**: Several utility bill calculators and blogs continue to display the July 2025 Schedule of Tariff ([S.R.O. 1161(I)/2025 dated 01-07-2025](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2025/07%20Jul/SRO%201161%2001-07-2025%20LESCO.pdf)), which listed higher variable charges for unprotected slabs above 300 units (e.g., Rs 37.99/kWh for 301–400 units and Rs 47.69/kWh above 700 units), an off-peak ToU rate of Rs 40.53/kWh, and per-connection fixed charges only above 300 units. Those rates were superseded on 12 February 2026 by [S.R.O. 279(I)/2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php).
3. **Base Energy Rate vs. Effective All-In Rate**: News headlines and consumer guides frequently quote an "effective per-unit rate" that bundles the base GoP variable energy charge with the Financing Cost (FC) Surcharge (Rs 3.23/kWh or Rs 0.43/kWh), Quarterly Tariff Adjustment (QTA), Fuel Price Adjustment (FPA), and 18% General Sales Tax (GST).
4. **Wholesale Purchase Price vs. Consumer-End Loss-Adjusted Component**: Under the 2026 Prosumer Regulations, press reports in February 2026 widely quoted a solar buyback rate of "Rs 10 to Rs 11 per unit" (or Rs 11.30/kWh), whereas NEPRA's formal statutory notifications ([07-01-2026 CPPA-G PPP Decision, Para 36](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/DISCOs/Rebasing%20Decisions/TRF-100%20CPPAG%20FOR%20APPROVAL%20OF%20POWER%20PURCHASE%20PRICE%20FORECAST%2007-01-2026%20437-76.PDF) and [S.R.O. 1501(I)/2026 dated 07-09-2026, Para 23](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2026/09%2520Sep/SRO%25201501%2528I%2529-2026%2520Dated%252007-09-2026.pdf)) explicitly establish the National Average Energy Purchase Price (NAEPP) at **Rs 8.13/kWh** (which translates to Rs 9.12/kWh at the consumer distribution level after accounting for 11.27% T&D losses).

### Statutory Definitions, Category Transitions, and Slab Application Rules

The exact legal definitions of Lifeline, Protected, and Unprotected residential consumers, as well as the mechanics of how slabs are applied and how consumers transition between categories, are codified in Annex-V (*Terms and Conditions of Tariff (For Supply of Electric Power to Consumers by Distribution Licensees)*) of the [NEPRA LESCO Tariff Determination dated 07-01-2026 (Pages 20–22)](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF) and Footnotes 1 through 4 of the Schedule of Tariff ([S.R.O. 1161(I)/2025, Page 4](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2025/07%20Jul/SRO%201161%2001-07-2025%20LESCO.pdf)):

1. **Lifeline Consumer Definition and Whole-Bill Application**: Under Part-I, Clause 9 and Part-II, Clause 2 of Annex-V ([07-01-2026 LESCO Rebasing Decision, p. 20 & p. 22](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF)), a *"Lifeline Consumer"* is a residential Non-Time-of-Use consumer having a single-phase electric connection with a **sanctioned load up to 1 kW** whose maximum consumption across the **last twelve (12) months and the current billing month is less than or equal to 100 units (≤100 kWh)**. Two consumption bands exist: up to 50 units (Rs 3.95/kWh) and 51–100 units (Rs 7.74/kWh). Critically, Footnote 2 of the Schedule of Tariff explicitly states that under Lifeline Tariff, **"there will be no slab benefit."** Therefore, a Lifeline consumer using 45 kWh pays $45 \times \text{Rs } 3.95 = \text{Rs } 177.75$, whereas a Lifeline consumer using 80 kWh pays **Rs 7.74/kWh on all 80 units** ($80 \times \text{Rs } 7.74 = \text{Rs } 619.20$), not incremental billing across the 1–50 band. If a Lifeline consumer's monthly consumption exceeds 100 kWh in any single month, the 12-month maximum exceeds 100 units and the consumer forfeits Lifeline status for the subsequent 12 months.
2. **Protected Consumer Definition and Incremental (Previous-Slab) Benefit**: Under Footnote 3 of the Schedule of Tariff ([S.R.O. 1161(I)/2025, p. 4](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2025/07%20Jul/SRO%201161%2001-07-2025%20LESCO.pdf)), to qualify as a Protected residential consumer, a connection must be a **Non-ToU residential consumer (sanctioned load under 5 kW)** consuming **≤200 kWh per month consistently for the past six (6) consecutive months**. Unlike Lifeline and Unprotected consumers, Footnote 3 stipulates that **"Residential Consumers will be given the benefit of one previous slab"** within the Protected category. Consequently, Protected consumers receive **incremental (telescopic) billing**:
   - A Protected consumer using **100 kWh or less** is billed at **Rs 10.54/kWh** on all units, plus the fixed charge for the 1–100 slab (**Rs 200/kW/month**).
   - A Protected consumer using between **101 and 200 kWh** (for example, 160 kWh) is billed **Rs 10.54/kWh on the first 100 units** and **Rs 13.01/kWh on the remaining 60 units** ($100 \times 10.54 + 60 \times 13.01 = \text{Rs } 1,834.60$), plus the fixed charge for the 101–200 slab (**Rs 300/kW/month**).
3. **Unprotected Consumer Definition and Whole-Bill (Landing-Slab) Application**: Any Non-ToU residential consumer (< 5 kW) who does not satisfy the Lifeline criteria or the 6-month consecutive ≤200 kWh Protected threshold falls into the **Unprotected** category. Under Footnote 4 of the Schedule of Tariff ([S.R.O. 1161(I)/2025, p. 4](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2025/07%20Jul/SRO%201161%2001-07-2025%20LESCO.pdf)), **"Under Un-Protected tariff there will be no previous slab benefit."** This rule— eliminated during the IMF structural reforms of 2023–2024 and maintained throughout 2025 and 2026—means that **whole-bill (landing-slab) pricing applies**: every single unit from unit 1 up to the final unit consumed in that billing cycle is charged at the single per-kWh rate of the highest slab reached. For example, an Unprotected consumer whose monthly meter reading shows 301 kWh does not pay tiered rates across 1–100, 101–200, and 201–300; instead, all 301 units are billed at the 301–400 slab rate of **Rs 36.46/kWh** ($301 \times 36.46 = \text{Rs } 10,974.46$), whereas consuming 300 kWh costs $300 \times 33.10 = \text{Rs } 9,930.00$—an immediate marginal cliff of **Rs 1,044.46** (plus a Rs 50/kW/month jump in fixed charges and taxes) for a single additional kilowatt-hour.
4. **Category Transition and 6-Month Rolling Window Mechanics**: Movement between Protected and Unprotected status is evaluated dynamically each month over a rolling 6-month look-back window ([BijliBills, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://bijlibills.com/lesco-slab-rates-and-tariff-guide/); [Dawn, 23-09-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.dawn.com/news/1944012/power-division-plans-steps-to-prevent-inflated-electricity-bills)). If a Protected consumer consumes **201 kWh** in a summer month (say, June), the consumer immediately transitions to **Unprotected (201–300 slab at Rs 33.10/kWh + Rs 3.23/kWh FC surcharge)** for June. Moreover, even if the consumer reduces consumption back to 150 kWh in July, August, September, October, and November, the presence of the single 201 kWh month in June within the preceding 6-month history keeps the consumer in the **Unprotected 101–200 slab (Rs 28.91/kWh + Rs 3.23/kWh FC surcharge)** for six consecutive subsequent billing months until six full months of ≤200 kWh consumption have been re-established.
5. **Pro-Rata Consumption Adjustment Rule for Extended Reading Cycles**: Under Part-I, Clause 1 of Annex-V ([07-01-2026 LESCO Rebasing Decision, p. 20](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF)) and Clause 6.1.1.1(a) of NEPRA's Consumer Service Manual (CSM Revised 2025), a billing month is defined as a period of 30 days. If LESCO records a meter reading beyond the number of days in that calendar month (e.g., 33 days in a 30-day month), LESCO is legally required to normalize the units on a pro-rata basis ($\text{Charged Units} = \text{Actual Units} \times \frac{\text{Days in Calendar Month}}{\text{Actual Reading Cycle Days}}$) to determine slab landing and Protected status eligibility.

### Fixed Charges Basis and Effective Date (S.R.O. 279(I)/2026)

Prior to 12 February 2026, residential fixed charges under [S.R.O. 1161(I)/2025](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2025/07%20Jul/SRO%201161%2001-07-2025%20LESCO.pdf) applied solely to Unprotected consumers exceeding 300 units per month and were denominated on a flat **per-connection (`Rs./Consumer/Month`)** basis (Rs 200 for 301–400, Rs 400 for 401–500, Rs 600 for 501–600, Rs 800 for 601–700, and Rs 1,000 above 700 units). Effective **12 February 2026**, under [S.R.O. 279(I)/2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php) and the [NEPRA Tariff Rationalization Decision dated 11-02-2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-100%20XWDISCOS%20AND%20KE%20RATIONALIZATION%20OF%20TARIFF%2011-02-2026%202935-57.pdf), the basis of residential fixed charges was changed to **per kilowatt of sanctioned load per month (`Rs./kW/Month`)** and extended to all Protected and Unprotected slabs (excluding Lifeline consumers):

- **Protected 1–100 Units**: **Rs 200/kW/month**
- **Protected 101–200 Units**: **Rs 300/kW/month**
- **Unprotected 1–100 Units**: **Rs 275/kW/month**
- **Unprotected 101–200 Units**: **Rs 300/kW/month**
- **Unprotected 201–300 Units**: **Rs 350/kW/month**
- **Unprotected 301–400 Units**: **Rs 400/kW/month**
- **Unprotected 401–500 Units**: **Rs 500/kW/month**
- **Unprotected >500 Units (501–600, 601–700, >700)**: **Rs 675/kW/month**

Because most single-phase connections in Lahore have a sanctioned load of **1 kW to 4 kW** (with 1 kW or 2 kW being the standard entry on older domestic demand notices), a 2 kW single-phase Unprotected household consuming 350 kWh now pays $2\text{ kW} \times \text{Rs } 400/\text{kW} = \text{Rs } 800/\text{month}$ in fixed charges (plus 18% GST), compared to Rs 200/month under the pre-February 2026 regime ([The Express Tribune, 14-02-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://tribune.com.pk/story/2592549/govt-notifies-new-power-tariffs-fixed-charges)). Under Part-II, Clause 5 of Annex-V ([07-01-2026 LESCO Rebasing Decision, p. 22](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF)), the Minimum Monthly Customer Charge (Rs 75 single-phase / Rs 150 three-phase) applies **only** to consumers on whom no monthly fixed charges are levied (i.e., Lifeline consumers with zero consumption); for all consumers subject to fixed charges, no separate minimum monthly charge is levied even if zero energy is consumed.

### Analysis of ToU Spread, Eligibility Mechanics, and Billing Demand Basis

Under the 12 February 2026 tariff rationalization ([S.R.O. 279(I)/2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php)), the Government of Pakistan maintained the residential ToU **Peak energy charge at Rs 46.85/kWh** while reducing the **Off-Peak energy charge by Rs 6.00/kWh from Rs 40.53/kWh to Rs 34.53/kWh** ([The Express Tribune, 14-02-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://tribune.com.pk/story/2592549/govt-notifies-new-power-tariffs-fixed-charges); [eBillPakistan, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://ebillpakistan.pk/lesco-tariff-rates-2026)). For battery energy-management systems in Lahore, this revision nearly doubled the base Peak-to-Off-Peak price spread from **Rs 6.32/kWh** (under the July 2025 – January 2026 schedule) to **Rs 12.32/kWh** before taxes, or **Rs 14.54/kWh** after applying 18% GST ($12.32 \times 1.18$). When comparing solar self-consumption or battery discharge during the 4-hour evening Peak window (saving Rs 46.85/kWh base + Rs 3.23/kWh FC + Rs 0.5194/kWh QTA + Rs 1.1086/kWh FCA + 18% GST ≈ **Rs 60.83/kWh** all-in) against exporting daytime solar to the grid under the 2026 Prosumer Net-Billing regime (credited at **Rs 8.13/kWh** NAEPP with zero tax credit), evening peak-shaving delivers a **7.48× higher economic return per kilowatt-hour** than daytime grid export.

With respect to eligibility and connection phase rules, Annex-V of the [LESCO CY 2026 Tariff Determination dated 07-01-2026 (Part-I Clause 11 and Part-II Clauses 3 & 4, pp. 21–22)](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF) establishes three binding rules:
1. **Sanctioned Load ≥ 5 kW (Mandatory 3-Phase ToU)**: All consumers having a sanctioned load of **5 kW and above** shall be provided a **3-Phase 400 Volts** supply and billed under the Time-of-Use (ToU) tariff (Category A-1(b)). Under Part-II, Clause 3, existing residential consumers with sanctioned load of 5 kW and above who were historically provided single-phase supply must be converted to three-phase supply by the distribution company.
2. **Sanctioned Load < 5 kW (Optional ToU)**: Under the statutory Note in the Schedule of Tariff ([S.R.O. 1161(I)/2025, p. 4](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2025/07%20Jul/SRO%201161%2001-07-2025%20LESCO.pdf)), *"Residential consumers having sanctioned load less than 5 kW can opt for ToU metering and shall be billed under ToU tariff."* However, once billed under ToU, they lose slab benefits and pay the Rs 675/kW/month fixed charge.
3. **Billing Demand Discrepancy for Fixed Charges**: Under [S.R.O. 279(I)/2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Distribution%20LESCO.php), the ToU fixed charge is **Rs 675/kW/month**. A notable discrepancy exists between the general statutory definition in NEPRA's *Terms and Conditions of Tariff* and secondary billing guides regarding how the billing kilowatts are determined:
   - **Primary Statutory Definition (25% Floor)**: Part-I, Clause 4 of Annex-V ([07-01-2026 LESCO Rebasing Decision, p. 20](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF)) defines *"Billing Demand"* as *"the **25% of the sanction load or Actual maximum demand recorded in a month, whichever is higher**, except in the case of agriculture tariff D2 where 'Billing Demand' shall mean the sanctioned load."*
   - **Secondary Billing Portals & Fixed Charge Practice (50% Floor vs. Full Sanctioned Load)**: Secondary tariff guides ([eBillPakistan, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://ebillpakistan.pk/lesco-tariff-rates-2026); [BijliBills, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://bijlibills.com/lesco-slab-rates-and-tariff-guide/)) state that for residential ToU connections (≥ 5 kW), fixed charges are calculated on **50% of sanctioned load or actual Maximum Demand Indicator (MDI) recorded during the month, whichever is higher** (mirroring the historical industrial/commercial 50% MDI floor prior to the June 2024 reduction to 25%), whereas for non-MDI or standard domestic connections LESCO's billing system often multiplies Rs 675 directly by the integer **Sanctioned Load (kW)** printed on the bill unless MDI exceeds sanctioned load. For EMS configuration, software should allow user selection between `100% Sanctioned Load`, `max(50% Sanctioned Load, MDI)`, and `max(25% Sanctioned Load, MDI)`.

Finally, regarding whether residential ToU peak hours differ from commercial or industrial peak hours: **they do not differ**. Part-I, Clause 10 of Annex-V ([07-01-2026 LESCO Rebasing Decision, p. 21](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF)) defines a single, uniform table of Peak and Off-Peak hours for the entire LESCO service territory across all ToU categories (Residential A-1(b), Commercial A-2(c), General Services A-3(b), Industrial B-1(b)/B-2(b)/B-3/B-4, Single Point Supply C, and Agricultural D-1(b)/D-2(b)):
- **December to February (inclusive)**: **5:00 PM to 9:00 PM** (Peak); remaining 20 hours (Off-Peak).
- **March to May (inclusive)**: **6:00 PM to 10:00 PM** (Peak); remaining 20 hours (Off-Peak).
- **June to August (inclusive)**: **7:00 PM to 11:00 PM** (Peak); remaining 20 hours (Off-Peak).
- **September to November (inclusive)**: **6:00 PM to 10:00 PM** (Peak); remaining 20 hours (Off-Peak).

### Detailed Mechanics and Statutory Basis of Each Bill Component

1. **Fuel Price Adjustment (FPA / FCA)**: Governed by Section 31(7) of the NEPRA Act and Annex-I of the [LESCO CY 2026 Tariff Determination dated 07-01-2026 (Pages 13–14)](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF), the monthly Fuel Charges Adjustment measures the variance between actual generation fuel cost per kWh and the monthly reference fuel cost established in Annex-IV of the determination. Critically, FPA is **always billed with a two-month lag** based on the **units consumed in that earlier reference month**, not the current month's units. For instance, in **October 2026 bills**, LESCO applies the **August 2026 FCA of +Rs 1.1086/kWh** (approved via [NEPRA Decision No. D-20276-94 & S.R.O. 1754(I)/2026 dated 07-10-2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/D-20276-94%20Decision%20FCA%20for%20XW-DISCOs%20August%202026%2007.10.2026.PDF)) multiplied by the consumer's **August 2026 kWh consumption**. On the LESCO bill, FPA attracts its own 18% GST (and 1.5% ED where levied), printed in the adjustment box. Lifeline consumers (≤100 units, ≤1 kW) and Prepaid consumers are exempt from both positive and negative FPA adjustments.
2. **Quarterly Tariff Adjustment (QTA)**: Governed by Annex-I-A of the [LESCO CY 2026 Tariff Determination dated 07-01-2026 (Pages 15–16)](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Ex-WAPDA%20DISCOS/2026/TRF-603%20&TRF%20604%20LESCO%20FOR%20DETERMINATION%20OF%20TARIFF%20REVISED%20ANNUAL%20REBASING%2007-01-2026%20237-44.PDF), QTA reconciles quarterly variations in Capacity Purchase Price (CPP), Variable O&M, Use of System Charges (UoSC), Market Operator Fee (MOF), and the impact of T&D losses on monthly FCA. Unlike FPA, QTA is applied to the **current billing month's imported kWh** over a three-month recovery window. For the three-month period of **September, October, and November 2026**, the applicable QTA notified via [S.R.O. 1501(I)/2026 dated 07-09-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2026/09%2520Sep/SRO%25201501%2528I%2529-2026%2520Dated%252007-09-2026.pdf) is **+Rs 0.5194/kWh** (replacing the Q1 CY 2026 negative QTA rebate of **−Rs 1.9857/kWh** that applied during June, July, and August 2026 under S.R.O. 953(I)/2026). Lifeline and Prepaid consumers are exempt from positive QTA charges (though NEPRA passes negative QTA relief to Protected and Unprotected consumers alike).
3. **Financing Cost (FC) Surcharge / Debt Servicing Surcharge (DSS)**: Instituted by the Federal Government under Section 31 of the NEPRA Act to service circular debt obligations parked in Power Holding Limited (PHL) and the June 2025 Rs 1.225 trillion commercial bank refinancing package, the FC Surcharge is levied on current-month imported units at **Rs 3.23/kWh** for Unprotected and ToU residential consumers and **Rs 0.43/kWh** for Protected residential consumers (≤200 units), while Lifeline consumers are exempt ([eBillPakistan, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://ebillpakistan.pk/lesco-tariff-rates-2026); [BijliBills, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://bijlibills.com/lesco-slab-rates-and-tariff-guide/)).
4. **Electricity Duty (ED) Discrepancy**: In June 2025, the Federal Ministry of Energy wrote to provincial Chief Ministers and announced the discontinuation of provincial Electricity Duty collection through DISCO bills starting **1 July 2025** ([LESCO Online Bills, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://lescoonlinebills.com.pk/taxes-on-lesco-bill/)). However, empirical inspection of 2026 LESCO bills and billing analyses ([Qaaf, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://qaaf.com.pk/understand-fuel-price-adjustment-fpa-in-lesco-bills/); [Oraan, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.oraan.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-taxes-on-lesco-bill)) confirms that **1.5% Electricity Duty** on variable energy charges (and on FPA) has continued to appear on many LESCO residential bills in Lahore where provincial finance statutes remained active. In the worked examples below, both the **Without ED (0%)** and **With ED (1.5%)** totals are explicitly shown so that EMS financial models can toggle the flag accurately.
5. **General Sales Tax (GST), PTV Fee, and Section 235 Withholding Income Tax**:
   - **GST (18%)**: Charged at **18%** under the Sales Tax Act, 1990 on the sum of Cost of Electricity (Variable Energy Charges), Fixed Charges, QTA, FC Surcharge, Electricity Duty (if levied), and Meter Rent, plus 18% on FPA (and ED on FPA).
   - **TV License Fee (PTV Fee)**: **Rs 0**. The historical Rs 35/month PTV fee was abolished across Pakistan effective **1 July 2025** ([BillAsaan, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://billaasaan.pk/electricity-bill-taxes-explained-pakistan); [LESCO Online Bills, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://lescoonlinebills.com.pk/taxes-on-lesco-bill/)).
   - **Income / Withholding Tax (Section 235 of Income Tax Ordinance, 2001)**: Domestic consumers appearing on FBR's Active Taxpayers List (**Filers / ATL**) pay **0% (Nil)** income tax on domestic electricity bills regardless of bill size. **Non-Filers** pay **0% (Nil)** if the total monthly bill is **less than Rs 25,000**, and **7.5%** of the total bill amount if the monthly bill is **Rs 25,000 or above** ([BillAsaan, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://billaasaan.pk/electricity-bill-taxes-explained-pakistan); [Oraan, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.oraan.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-taxes-on-lesco-bill)).

### Deep-Dive Analysis of Net-Billing Mechanics, Buyback Discrepancy, and Grandfathering

#### 1. How the Export / Buyback Rate is Calculated and Why Sources Cite Rs 8.13 vs. Rs 10–11/kWh
Under Regulation 14(1) of the [NEPRA (Prosumer) Regulations, 2026 (S.R.O. 251(I)/2026, Page 7)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/NEPRA%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%2520%2528SRO%2520251%2528I%25292026%2529%252009-02-26.PDF):
- The distribution licensee (LESCO) bills the prosumer at the end of each billing cycle strictly for the **gross electricity supplied (imported) by the licensee** to the prosumer at the applicable consumer-end tariff (e.g., Rs 46.85/kWh Peak and Rs 34.53/kWh Off-Peak, plus Rs 3.23/kWh FC Surcharge, Rs 0.5194/kWh QTA, FPA, and 18% GST).
- Simultaneously, the licensee raises an invoice/credit for the **electricity supplied (exported) by the prosumer to the licensee** at the **National Average Energy Purchase Price (NAEPP)**.
- Under Regulation 14(2), if the monetary value of the prosumer's exported kWh exceeds the monetary bill for imported electricity, the **net rupee amount is credited against the prosumer's next monthly billing cycle or paid by the licensee to the prosumer quarterly**. Crucially, **1:1 kWh unit offsetting is abolished for new prosumers**: daytime exported solar kWh can no longer cancel out nighttime off-peak imported kWh on a unit-for-unit basis.

Why do primary NEPRA documents state **Rs 8.13/kWh** while major newspapers ([Dawn, 10-02-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.dawn.com/news/1972145); [Business Recorder, 10-02-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.brecorder.com/news/40406354); [The Express Tribune, 03-04-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://tribune.com.pk/story/2600812)) reported **"Rs 10 to Rs 11 per unit"** (or Rs 11.30/kWh)? The exact mathematical breakdown is documented in Paragraph 36 (Page 17) of [NEPRA's CPPA-G CY 2026 Power Purchase Price Forecast Decision dated 07-01-2026](https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/DISCOs/Rebasing%20Decisions/TRF-100%20CPPAG%20FOR%20APPROVAL%20OF%20POWER%20PURCHASE%20PRICE%20FORECAST%2007-01-2026%20437-76.PDF) and re-confirmed verbatim in Paragraph 23 (Page 7) of [S.R.O. 1501(I)/2026 dated 07-09-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2026/09%2520Sep/SRO%25201501%2528I%2529-2026%2520Dated%252007-09-2026.pdf):
- **National Average Power Purchase Price (NAPPP)**: **Rs 25.32/kWh** (comprising Rs 8.13/kWh Energy Purchase Price + Rs 16.05/kWh Capacity Purchase Price + Rs 1.14/kWh Transmission/Market Operator charges at the wholesale NTDC level).
- **National Average Energy Purchase Price (NAEPP)**: **Rs 8.13/kWh** (the pure wholesale fuel + variable O&M energy cost at the CPPA-G pool level, which rises to **Rs 9.12/kWh** at the consumer distribution level after adjusting for 11.27% T&D losses, and stood near Rs 10–11/kWh under earlier FY 2024-25 pool estimates cited informally to journalists in February 2026).
- In Paragraph 23 of [S.R.O. 1501(I)/2026 dated 07-09-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/tariff/Tariff/Notifications/2026/09%2520Sep/SRO%25201501%2528I%2529-2026%2520Dated%252007-09-2026.pdf), NEPRA left zero ambiguity by ruling explicitly: *"Regarding adjustments of Net Metering / Net Billing units, the Authority had already determined the National Average Power Purchase Price (NAPPP) as Rs.25.32/kWh and National Average Energy Purchase Price (NAEPP) as Rs.8.13/kWh in the PPP decision dated 07.01.2026."* Thus, **Rs 8.13/kWh** is the legally binding NAEPP buyback rate for post-9 February 2026 prosumers in CY 2026.

#### 2. Grandfathering Cutoff Date, Contract Duration, Renewal, and Legal Challenges
When [S.R.O. 251(I)/2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/NEPRA%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%2520%2528SRO%2520251%2528I%25292026%2529%252009-02-26.PDF) was originally published on 9 February 2026, Regulation 21(2) preserved the old Rs 25.32/kWh rate until contract expiry *only* for quarterly net excess cash-outs while subjecting existing solar users' monthly billing to Regulation 14 (net billing). Following an immediate public outcry, a directive from Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on 12 February 2026 to protect existing ~466,000 solar users, and a writ petition before the **Lahore High Court (LHC)** heard by Justice Abid Hussain Chattah on 14 February 2026 ([Dawn, 15-02-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.dawn.com/news/1973290)), NEPRA published a draft amendment on 20 February 2026 and notified the final **First Amendment via [S.R.O. 547(I)/2026 on 02-04-2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/S.R.O%2520547%25281%2529%25202026%2520notification%2520amendment%2520in%2520NEPRA%2520%2528Prosumer%2529%2520Regulations%252002-04-2026.PDF)**:
- **Cutoff Date**: Agreements validly executed **prior to 9 February 2026** (`< 09-02-2026`) are fully grandfathered because S.R.O. 547(I)/2026 Section 1(2) states: *"They shall come into force at once and shall be deemed to have taken effect on and from the 9th day of February, 2026."*
- **Grandfathered Rights & Contract Duration**: Existing net-metering prosumers retain both **1:1 kWh monthly unit netting** against Off-Peak imports and the **Rs 25.32/kWh (NAPPP)** buyback rate until the expiry of their existing **7-year agreement term**.
- **Loss of Grandfathering via Modification or Renewal**: Under the proviso to amended Regulation 21(2) ([S.R.O. 547(I)/2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/S.R.O%2520547%25281%2529%25202026%2520notification%2520amendment%2520in%2520NEPRA%2520%2528Prosumer%2529%2520Regulations%252002-04-2026.PDF)), grandfathering applies only so long as the agreement is **not modified or altered in any substantive manner that changes the maximum electrical output** of the distributed generation system. Expanding inverter/panel capacity or renewing the contract upon the expiry of the 7-year term (under Regulation 21(3)) automatically transitions the consumer to a **5-year Net-Billing agreement** at NAEPP (**Rs 8.13/kWh**).

### What "Green Meter" Actually Means in LESCO Usage and Source Disagreements

In Lahore's residential solar market, the term **"green meter"** has two overlapping meanings that occasionally cause confusion between consumers, solar installers, and utility engineers:
1. **Primary LESCO & Consumer Meaning (Solar Net-Metering Bidirectional Meter)**: Historically, when a Lahore resident applied for solar net metering under the 2015 Regulations, LESCO required replacing the unidirectional three-phase ToU meter with a specialized **three-phase four-quadrant bidirectional static digital meter** capable of recording four distinct energy registers: `Import Peak`, `Import Off-Peak`, `Export Peak`, and `Export Off-Peak`. Because LESCO's approved vendor meters for net metering featured a distinctive green faceplate/enclosure and served renewable ("green") solar energy, both LESCO sub-divisional offices and consumers universally referred to any net-metering bidirectional meter as a **"green meter"** ([The Express Tribune, Sep 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://tribune.com.pk/story/2567890); [SolarWorks, 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.solarworks.pk/blog/pakistan-electricity-unit-rates-2026/)).
2. **Why Some Sources Say "Green Meters Are Banned/Discontinued"**: In **September 2025**, LESCO formally halted the procurement and installation of standalone non-communicating bidirectional "green meters" and replaced them with **three-phase AMI (Advanced Metering Infrastructure) smart meters** (priced around Rs 42,000–45,000 on solar demand notices). An AMI smart meter is inherently a **three-phase, Time-of-Use, bidirectional net-metering/net-billing meter** equipped with an embedded cellular (GPRS/4G) or PLC communication module that transmits real-time import/export registers to LESCO's central data center without human meter readers. Thus, while colloquial usage still calls any solar export meter a "green meter," technically LESCO now installs **3-phase AMI bidirectional smart meters** for all new prosumer connections.
3. **Relation to Phase, ToU, and Net Metering**: Because both the 2015 Net Metering Regulations and Regulation 2(1)(v) of the [NEPRA (Prosumer) Regulations, 2026 (S.R.O. 251(I)/2026)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://nepra.org.pk/Legislation/3-Reg/3.35%2520NEPRA%2520%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%25202026/NEPRA%2520Prosumer%2520Regulations%2520%2528SRO%2520251%2528I%25292026%2529%252009-02-26.PDF) restrict prosumer eligibility to **three-phase (400V or 11kV)** connections, every residential net-metering/net-billing meter in LESCO is simultaneously:
   - **Three-Phase (400V)**,
   - **Time-of-Use (ToU)** (recording Peak and Off-Peak windows), and
   - **Bidirectional** (recording imported and exported kWh separately).
   Conversely, a consumer who requires **only ToU billing** (≥5 kW load without solar) needs a **three-phase ToU digital or AMI meter** (which does not have export billing enabled unless a Prosumer Agreement is executed), whereas a consumer who requires **net metering / net billing** must have a **three-phase connection with a LESCO-approved bidirectional AMI smart meter** (or legacy bidirectional "green meter" if commissioned before September 2025, or two separate unidirectional meters under Regulation 12(1) of S.R.O. 251(I)/2026).

## Conclusions and Solar/Battery Energy-Management System (EMS) Configuration Synthesis

To configure a residential solar and battery energy-management product in Lahore accurately, the software engine must decouple consumer profiles into three distinct optimization regimes:

1. **Post-9 February 2026 Three-Phase Prosumers (Net-Billing Regime under S.R.O. 251(I)/2026)**:
   - **Self-Consumption Priority over Grid Export**: Because daytime grid exports earn only **Rs 8.13/kWh** (NAEPP) with zero tax or surcharge offset while grid imports cost **Rs 45.77/kWh** all-in during Off-Peak hours ($\text{Rs } [34.53 + 3.23 + 0.5194 + 1.1086] \times 1.18$ before ED) and **Rs 60.31/kWh** all-in during Peak hours ($\text{Rs } [46.85 + 3.23 + 0.5194 + 1.1086] \times 1.18$ before ED), every kilowatt-hour of solar generation stored in a home battery and discharged during the evening Peak window saves **Rs 52.18/kWh net** compared to exporting that unit at noon.
   - **Automated Seasonal Peak-Window Switching**: The EMS firmware must automatically shift its 4-hour evening battery discharge schedule across the four statutory LESCO seasons: **17:00–21:00** (Dec–Feb), **18:00–22:00** (Mar–May), **19:00–23:00** (Jun–Aug), and **18:00–22:00** (Sep–Nov).
2. **Pre-9 February 2026 Grandfathered Three-Phase Prosumers (Legacy Net-Metering Regime under S.R.O. 547(I)/2026)**:
   - Until the expiry of their 7-year contract term, grandfathered users retain **1:1 kWh monthly netting** between daytime solar exports and Off-Peak grid imports, plus **Rs 25.32/kWh (NAPPP)** on quarterly net surplus units. For these users, the battery need only cover the **4-hour Peak window** (since Peak imports cannot be offset 1:1 by daytime Off-Peak exports) and grid outages, while daytime excess solar can continue to bank 1:1 against nighttime Off-Peak air-conditioning loads.
3. **Single-Phase Non-ToU Slab Consumers (< 5 kW, Zero-Export / Hybrid Self-Consumption)**:
   - Because single-phase connections (< 5 kW) are ineligible for a prosumer net-billing agreement without upgrading to a 3-phase meter, single-phase hybrid solar-plus-battery systems must operate with **hard zero-export limiting** (to prevent unidirectional digital or AMI meters from billing reverse current as forward consumption).
   - For single-phase consumers near the **200 kWh/month Protected threshold**, the EMS should implement a **Cumulative Monthly Grid-Import Guardrail**: keeping monthly grid import at or below **195 kWh** preserves the Protected tariff (**Rs 10.54–13.01/kWh** + Rs 0.43/kWh FC surcharge) and avoids triggering a six-month penalty into the Unprotected tariff (**Rs 28.91–33.10/kWh** + Rs 3.23/kWh FC surcharge). Similarly, for Unprotected consumers, shaving monthly grid imports just below whole-bill slab boundaries (100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, and 700 kWh) captures massive step-function bill reductions across the entire month's consumption.
